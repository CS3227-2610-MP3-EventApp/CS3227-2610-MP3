Converge is a convention and event platform with three distinct workspaces. The columns below show **product involvement**, not which teammate writes the code. A dash means that role has no direct user-facing feature in that story. The role named in the story is the primary stakeholder; another role appears where it supplies or consumes the same workflow. Priority is a planning proposal, not a course rule. For a mixed feature group, use the story-level label below.

### What F10 shared foundation covers

F10 groups capabilities used by all three workspaces. **Identity** means verifying who signed in. **Permissions** mean the server checks whether that person can read or change the specific attendee, event, or business record requested. An organiser's authority applies only to assigned events. A business member's authority applies only to their business, across that business's event applications and preparation workspaces. Selecting a different screen never grants access. The exact business member roles and invitation policy still need a team decision.

**Bounded AI jobs** are requests to the SoC LLM with a defined owner, permitted data and tools, limits on time and usage, visible progress/failure, cancellation between steps, and validation of results before display. An AI job may save an authorised draft but cannot decide an application, publish an announcement, or take an external action on its own.

“Start F10 with F01” means build the smallest shared slice for the first organiser-to-attendee journey: sign-in, event-scoped organiser checks, published programme access, and server-side denial of unauthorised writes. Add business membership checks as F06 begins and AI-job controls as F03/F04/F06 begin. The later exact-action approval part of F10 is needed only if F08/F09 sends a message or performs another external action.

**Build sequence:** Start with F10 sign-in and event-scoped permissions while building F01 programme creation, publication, and browsing. Add F10 business access checks with F06, then F10 AI-job controls when the first AI feature is built. Deliver F02/F03 and F06/F04 as role journeys, followed by F05 integration. This is a dependency guide, not a strict serial schedule. P2 work begins only after P0/P1 acceptance criteria, security checks, and deployment are in place. F10 exact-action approval moves into the same phase as any real external send or purchase action.

## Decisions for team review

1. Confirm whether P0 + P1 is achievable by the deadline, especially each role's AI slice (F03, F04, F06). Keep P2 as later-phase scope unless the team explicitly expands the first release.
2. Confirm initial F04 submissions are **business applications only**; speaker submissions need a separate submitter workflow and decision. Confirm the rubric and what decision reasons applicants can see.
3. Define attendee hard constraints and unknown accessibility handling, plus programme conflict policy, before accepting AI plans or publishing items.
4. Confirm identity provider, organiser provisioning, deployment targets, SoC LLM data policy, and numerical rate/usage limits. Use separate development and production environments and team-managed deployment automation.
5. Assign a feature approver and independent reviewer, then turn agreed stories into acceptance criteria, security tests, and implementation evidence. Record SDD and multi-agent development handoffs and reflection separately.

Across all AI stories, use authorised data, validate structured output and factual references, limit model/tool calls, and preserve manual workflows on failure. Treat submissions and external sources as untrusted; prompt injection must not change rubric outcomes, expose private data, publish content, or bypass exact-action approval. These are cross-cutting requirements, detailed in [security rules](../workflow/sdd/security.md), rather than additional end-user roles.
